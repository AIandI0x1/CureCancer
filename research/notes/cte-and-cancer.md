# Research note: chronic traumatic encephalopathy (CTE) and cancer — structural similarities and patterns

Literature review, 2026-11-27. Docs only: no data, no code, no patient information. **Not medical advice.**
Every source below was checked against its PubMed record (PMID listed). Numbers are quoted from the abstracts.

## Bottom line

1. **No peer-reviewed study tests CTE itself against cancer.** A PubMed title/abstract search for CTE combined with cancer, tumour, neoplasm or glioma finds no study of that question. This is absence of evidence, not evidence of absence.
2. **What exists is cancer data on the exposure** (repetitive head impacts, traumatic brain injury), not on autopsy-confirmed CTE. Contact-sport cohorts mostly show *lower* cancer and all-cause mortality alongside *higher* neurodegenerative mortality.
3. **Brain-tumour risk after head trauma is inconsistent**: one small short-term study finds it higher; a nationwide cohort finds it lower after five years.
4. **The real overlap is mechanistic and shared with neurodegeneration in general**, not specific to CTE: tau and microtubules, the Pin1 prolyl isomerase, protein aggregation, DNA damage and senescence, cell-cycle re-entry, chronic inflammation, and the inverse comorbidity between neurodegeneration and cancer.

## 1. What CTE is (established)

- Neurodegenerative tauopathy linked to repetitive head impacts or blast exposure. Pathognomonic lesion: hyperphosphorylated tau (p-tau) in neurons and astroglia around small blood vessels at the depths of cortical sulci, in an irregular pattern. Diagnosed only post-mortem. [1][2]
- In life, the research diagnosis is traumatic encephalopathy syndrome (TES). [3]
- Case series of 202 donated brains of American football players: CTE in 177 (87%), including 110 of 111 NFL players. Brain-bank donors are strongly selection-biased; bias-adjusted prevalence work appeared in 2026. [4][6]
- Young contact-sport athletes (152 donors, mean age 23): CTE in 63 (41.4%), mostly mild. [5]
- Cryo-EM: CTE tau filaments have a fold distinct from Alzheimer's and Pick's disease. A different beta-helix conformation creates a hydrophobic cavity that encloses an additional density not connected to tau, suggesting a cofactor. [7]
- Microglial activation (CD68) is associated with duration of head-impact exposure and with p-tau. [8] TDP-43 co-pathology occurs in a subset. [9]

## 2. Direct evidence on cancer (exposure-level, not CTE-level)

| Study | Population | Cancer-relevant finding |
|---|---|---|
| Lehman 2012 [10] | 3,439 NFL players | All-cause SMR 0.53; neurodegenerative SMR 2.83 (underlying cause) / 3.26 (multiple cause) |
| Mackay 2019 [11] | 7,676 Scottish soccer players vs 23,028 controls | Lung-cancer mortality HR 0.53 (0.40–0.70); neurodegenerative primary cause sHR 3.45 (2.11–5.62) |
| Nguyen 2019 [12] | 3,419 NFL vs 2,708 MLB players | Neurodegenerative HR 2.99, cardiovascular HR 2.40; no cancer excess reported in abstract |
| Phelps 2022 [13] | 447 college football players | All-cause SMR 0.54; lung cancer SMR 0.26; brain/nervous-system cancer SMR 3.82 (1.04–9.77) |
| Luster 2026 [14] | 19,824 NFL players | All-cause SMR 0.70; neurodegenerative SMR 3.94 (3.38–4.56) |
| Chen 2012 [15] | 5,007 TBI vs 25,035 comparison (Taiwan) | Malignant brain tumour within 3 years HR 4.67 (1.84–11.83); 9 cases in each cohort |
| Munch 2015 [16] | 8.2 million Danes | Malignant astrocytic tumour: 1–4 y RR 1.14 (n.s.); 5+ y RR 0.68; TBI 5+ y RR 0.32 (0.10–0.75) |

Reading: the short-term excess after TBI is plausibly explained by tumours found on post-injury imaging (reverse causation / surveillance). The long-term Danish data point the other way, and the single college-cohort excess rests on very few deaths with a wide interval.

Context — inverse comorbidity: cancer survivors had lower Alzheimer's risk (HR 0.67, Framingham) [17]; in >1 million residents of Northern Italy, cancer risk was halved in Alzheimer's dementia and Alzheimer's risk was 35% lower after cancer [18]; genes up-regulated in Alzheimer's, Parkinson's and schizophrenia overlap significantly with genes down-regulated in lung, prostate and colorectal cancer, and vice versa [19]. Reviews: [20][21].

## 3. Structural and mechanistic overlap

| Theme | CTE / TBI side | Cancer side | Strength |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tau and microtubules | Tau is the CTE aggregate, with its own fold [7] | Low tau associated with paclitaxel complete response in breast cancer (OR 3.7) [25]; IDH status epigenetically controls tau (MAPT) in glioma, and tau impairs EGFR/NF-κB/TAZ-driven mesenchymal transformation [26]; review [27] | Moderate |
| Pin1 prolyl isomerase | cis P-tau appears acutely after TBI in humans and mice, spreads, and precedes tauopathy; a cis antibody blocks it in mice [24] | Pin1 over-expressed/activated in many cancers, down-regulated/inactivated in Alzheimer's [22][23] | Moderate |
| Protein aggregation | Distinct tau conformers propagate between cells [7][24] | Destabilized mutant p53 self-assembles through an aggregation-nucleating sequence and co-aggregates wild-type p53, p63 and p73 [31][32] | Analogy |
| DNA damage and senescence | Repeated mild TBI in mice: DNA damage response at 24 h, senescence signature by 7 days [35]; clearing senescent glia prevents tau pathology in mice [36] | Senescent cells are a hallmark dimension of cancer [39] | Indirect |
| Cell-cycle re-entry | Post-mitotic neurons that re-enter the cycle die [33]; cell-cycle inhibition is neuroprotective after TBI in rats [34] | Loss of cell-cycle control is a core cancer hallmark [39] | Indirect |
| Chronic inflammation | Microglial activation tracks exposure and p-tau [8] | Tumour-promoting inflammation is a cancer hallmark [39]; neurodegeneration hallmarks include inflammation [38] | Shared, non-specific |
| Somatic mutation | Not measured in CTE | Somatic SNVs accumulate in single Alzheimer's neurons [37] | Untested in CTE |

**A cautionary example.** A 2003 study placed tau's microtubule-binding repeats inside the microtubule at the taxol site [28]; near-atomic cryo-EM in 2018 placed tau along the outer protofilament surface [29]. An attractive structural overlap was revised by better structural data. Microtubule stabilizers have been tested in tauopathy models (epothilone D) [30].

## 4. Hypotheses (not findings)

- **H1.** The non-tau molecule inside the CTE filament cavity [7] could reflect a small-molecule/lipid interaction surface also relevant to aggregation-prone oncoproteins such as mutant p53. No data.
- **H2.** Pin1 loss / cis P-tau after head impacts might shift brain tissue toward the neurodegeneration side of the inverse-comorbidity axis, predicting lower glioma rates with exposure. Munch 2015 is weakly consistent; untested in CTE.
- **H3.** Persistent post-impact senescence and its inflammatory secretome could alter brain-tumour risk. Unknown.
- **H4.** The perivascular location of CTE lesions resembles the tumour vascular niche. Purely analogical.

## 5. What would falsify a claimed CTE–cancer overlap

1. Autopsy-confirmed CTE donors show the same cancer incidence as matched non-CTE donors after adjusting for smoking, BMI and socioeconomic status.
2. Single-cell or spatial profiling of CTE lesions shows Pin1, cell-cycle and DNA-repair genes *not* moving in the inverse-comorbidity direction.
3. Large prospective head-impact cohorts with dose measures show flat glioma risk (would refute H2).
4. Once identified, the CTE cavity molecule shows no affinity for p53 aggregation-nucleating segments (would refute H1).

## 6. What this note does NOT show

- It reports no new data or analysis; it is a structured reading of published abstracts.
- Exposure (head impacts) is not disease (CTE); cohort ratios are confounded by fitness, smoking, socioeconomics and competing risks.
- Several mechanistic links come from Alzheimer's disease or mouse TBI models, not human CTE.

## Open questions

- CTE cannot yet be diagnosed in life, which blocks cohort studies of CTE and cancer.
- Do CTE neurons carry extra somatic mutations, as Alzheimer's neurons do?
- Is athletes' lower cancer mortality biology, or fitness, smoking and competing risks?
- What is the non-tau molecule inside the CTE filament?
